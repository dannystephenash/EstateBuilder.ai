REQUIRED APPLICATIONS ▼
Determines which Planning Act applications are required (OPA, ZBA, SPA, MV, COA) based on as-of-right zoning vs. proposed massing. Reads building data directly from your rendered model — no manual inputs needed. Uses Planning Act R.S.O. 1990 c.P.13 statutory thresholds and Toronto Municipal Code Chapter 415 (Site Plan Control).
Sources: Planning Act R.S.O. 1990, c.P.13: §17 (OPA), §22 (Privately-initiated OPA), §34 (Zoning By-law Amendment), §41 (Site Plan Control), §45 (Minor Variance / Committee of Adjustment). Statutory decision timelines: §17(40) — 120 days for OPA; §34(11) — 120 days for ZBA; §22(7.0.2) — 120 days privately-initiated OPA; §45(7) — appeal window 20 days for MV. SPA timelines per municipal delegated authority (Toronto Municipal Code §415-7: 30 days for delegated, council approval longer). Pre-application consultation: §17(7.4), §34(10.0.1), §41(3.1) — Toronto requires PAC for ZBA, OPA, and SPA. MV "minor" threshold is statutory but undefined — courts apply 4-test (De Gasperis v. Vaughan, 1999): minor in nature, desirable, intent of by-law/plan maintained. The 10% input here is a planning-practice rule of thumb, not statutory.
INCLUSIONARY ZONING ▼
Estimates affordable-unit obligation under City of Toronto Inclusionary Zoning By-law 89-2022. Applies to projects ≥100 residential units within Protected Major Transit Station Areas (PMTSA). IZ rate phases up from 5% to 22% by 2030; the rate is editable below to reflect your project's location-specific schedule.
Sources: City of Toronto Inclusionary Zoning By-law 89-2022 (consolidated). Authority: Planning Act §35.2. Trigger: residential development ≥100 units within a Protected Major Transit Station Area as designated in OPA 558. Affordable rent definition: ≤80% of CMHC Average Market Rent by unit type (low-income tier ≤60% AMR; moderate ≤80%; middle ≤100%). 99-year affordability period required by By-law 89-2022. AMR figures: refer to current CMHC Rental Market Report for the relevant CMA. The default ($2,200) is illustrative only — replace with the current CMHC figure for your unit-type and CMA before relying on this estimate.
WALKABILITY + TRANSIT ▼
Distance from site to nearest TTC subway/Line 5/Line 6 station (bundled coordinates from TTC public station list) plus amenity counts within 500m / 1km via Mapbox Search Box API. Reports objective distance metrics, NOT a proprietary Walk Score.
Sources: TTC subway/Line 5 station coordinates from publicly published TTC station list (Toronto Transit Commission, ttc.ca/routes-and-schedules). Amenity counts use Mapbox Search Box API /category endpoint — categories: grocery, restaurant, school, park, medical_clinic, pharmacy. Distance computed using haversine formula on WGS84 great-circle (accurate to within ~0.5% over distances under 5 km). The "Walk Score" trademark is owned by Walk Score Inc. and not used here.
WATERMAIN CAPACITY CHECK ▼
Compares estimated site water demand against the carrying capacity of the nearest watermain. Run SCAN INFRASTRUCTURE first to populate pipe diameters from Toronto Open Data CKAN; otherwise this tool will report "no pipe data available".
Sources: Domestic per-capita water use: MECP Design Guidelines for Drinking-Water Systems (formerly MOE Design Guidelines for Water Supply). Toronto residential range 225–450 L/cap/day; default 350 reflects typical Toronto household. Peaking factor 2.5 is industry-standard for max-day demand (range 2.0–3.0 per AWWA M32). Capacity = velocity × cross-sectional area = v × π × (d/2)². Design velocity 1.5 m/s is the standard upper limit for ductile-iron mains per AWWA C150. Pipe diameter pulled from Toronto Open Data CKAN watermain dataset (resource ID: 2024 Watermains, fields PIPE_SIZE, MATERIAL, INSTALL_YEAR). A formal Functional Servicing Report by a P.Eng. is required for development application — this tool is a screening estimate only.
SHADOW STUDY ▼
Solar position and cast-shadow length using NOAA Solar Position Algorithm (analytical). Toronto Tall Building Design Guidelines target ≤50% net new shadow on adjacent parks/open space between 9:00–18:00 on March 21 (spring equinox).
Sources: NOAA Earth System Research Lab — Solar Position Algorithm (Spencer 1971 series for declination, NOAA equation of time). City of Toronto Tall Building Design Guidelines (2013, updated 2024) §3.3 — shadow on parks. Project latitude derived from sitemap centre; if no map fix, defaults to Toronto City Hall (43.6532°N, −79.3832°W). Outputs assume flat ground; cast lengths are exact for the building's tallest point only.
